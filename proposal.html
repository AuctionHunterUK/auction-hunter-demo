Section 1

Executive summary

AuctionSavvy is a working demonstration of a personalised discovery layer for auction buyers. It helps dealers and collectors identify relevant lots quickly, see where and when they are being sold, and continue to Easy Live Auction for the official catalogue, registration and bidding.

The boundary is deliberate and fixed. AuctionSavvy is not a catalogue, marketplace or bidding platform. Registration and bidding remain with Easy Live Auction, transactions remain within its auctioneer relationships, and Easy Live Auction retains catalogue control throughout. AuctionSavvy’s sole role is to generate qualified journeys into Easy Live Auction — introducing relevant buyers and helping existing members find suitable lots more efficiently.

This is a founder-led proposal, to be developed only under an authorised data arrangement with Easy Live Auction. The suggested starting point is a private walkthrough of the working system, followed — if there is mutual interest — by a data and technical discussion and a controlled pilot with jointly agreed success measures.

Section 2

What is running today

779 matching lots in the current briefing
105 auction houses represented
490 houses on the UK map
Nightly automated updates since 5 July 2026

Snapshot reviewed 25 July 2026. These figures show present working scale; they are not claims of user adoption or commercial performance.

The demonstration is a working, automatically refreshed system rather than a mock-up. It currently provides:

  • A morning briefing — matching lots grouped into Local Auctions, UK-Wide Today and UK-Wide Later, so relevance, urgency and geography can be assessed at a glance.
  • Featured searches — broad demonstration interests build the briefing; in a member service, each member would set their own.
  • Refined search — private search phrases compared against the current briefing in one action, using transparent keyword matching.
  • Planned auction-day reminders — a bell on each lot card demonstrates how a signed-in member could request an auction-day email containing the official Easy Live Auction lot link, with an optional second reminder shortly before the auction begins. No reminder is created in the demonstration; a live service would depend on an agreed data arrangement.
  • A UK auction-house map — houses explored by location and forthcoming sale timing, with links to official auction pages.
  • A working Chrome companion — a privately used browser extension reveals auction-house addresses and postcodes while browsing Easy Live Auction. Any wider distribution would be discussed transparently with Easy Live Auction.
  • Direct continuation to Easy Live Auction — every displayed lot links to its official listing for verification, registration and bidding.

Alongside the web demonstration, a working private AI-assisted sourcing trial is already operating successfully. At 7am each morning, it compares test wanted-item requirements held in a dedicated email folder with newly loaded listings, then emails a prioritised list of possible matches. Each suggestion includes a brief explanation of the match and a direct link to the official Easy Live Auction item. In a member-facing version, users would normally add, edit and remove wanted items directly inside AuctionSavvy; importing relevant requirements from a message could be an optional shortcut rather than the main method. Strong and possible matches would be prioritised, uncertain cases marked for manual review, and the service would not contact customers or place bids. Any message import would use only necessary product requirements—not complete emails or unrelated personal details—with suitable privacy, security and authorised-data arrangements.

The demonstration is private and invitation-only, and uses information gathered from publicly accessible Easy Live Auction pages for proof-of-concept purposes. A live service would operate solely on the authorised basis set out in Section 6.

Section 3

The problem it solves

Auction catalogues contain a continuous flow of opportunity, but relevance is highly personal. A dealer may want stock within particular categories, dimensions or locations; a collector may follow a narrow subject, maker or period. Both have limited time to repeat broad searches across many houses and sale dates.

The problem is not a lack of catalogue information — it is the daily effort of deciding what deserves attention. Relevant lots are dispersed across houses and dates, broad interests may be described using many different catalogue terms, and location, urgency and collection practicality matter as much as the object itself. AuctionSavvy addresses that decision stage: it turns a large, changing catalogue into a manageable daily starting point, with Easy Live Auction as the destination.

Section 4

What Easy Live Auction gains

Qualified referrals Every promising lot leads to its official Easy Live Auction page, not to a competing transaction.
Timely return to auctions of interest Don’t miss an auction—or a lot—you have already marked as interesting. Optional reminders could bring motivated buyers back to Easy Live Auction at useful moments.
New buyer introductions AuctionSavvy can be promoted directly to relevant dealer and collector audiences.
Wider lot exposure Suitable lots could reach buyers beyond their usual local area or followed houses.
Measurable value Referral measurement can show which discovery journeys produce genuine engagement, subject to agreement.

Easy Live Auction already provides Auction Alerts, a Watch List and advanced catalogue search. AuctionSavvy complements these rather than reproducing them: its distinction is the combined morning workflow, in which matches across a member’s interests and locations arrive in one briefing, prioritised by relevance, urgency and proximity.

Intelligent Featured Search could let a member enter one broad interest—such as ceramics—then propose related concepts including pottery, porcelain, stoneware and studio pottery while recognising relevant catalogue language beyond exact keyword matches. The interpretation would remain visible and editable, so the member could accept useful suggestions, remove irrelevant ones and retain control of the briefing.

Section 5

Who else benefits

Dealers and collectors

Members begin the day with a concise view: new lots within chosen interests, nearby sales happening soon, UK-wide lots that may justify collection or delivery, and possible matches for a customer request — each with a direct link to the official information needed before bidding. AuctionSavvy provides prioritisation, not valuation advice or a substitute for the buyer’s own due diligence.

Auction houses and sellers

Suitable lots could reach relevant bidders who do not already follow that house or routinely browse that sale. Looking further ahead, prospective sellers could be guided towards appropriate houses and an official valuation or consignment inquiry — with suitability, acceptance and valuation remaining entirely matters for the house.

Overseas buyers and UK buying tours

Overseas dealers, designers and buying-tour visitors could identify relevant UK sales before a sourcing trip and include auction visits in their itineraries — introducing informed international buyers to houses they might not otherwise encounter. Eligibility, payment, collection, shipping and export would remain subject to official terms and the relevant providers.

Professional sourcing advisers are another relevant audience. Art and antiques advisers, auction buying agents and interior sourcing specialists may manage several precise client briefs at once. AuctionSavvy could help them monitor relevant lots, prioritise possible matches and continue to each official Easy Live Auction listing—reducing repetitive research while leaving assessment and purchase advice with the professional.

Section 6

Responsible data use

Rights in catalogue descriptions, photographs and related material remain with Easy Live Auction, participating auctioneers and their respective rights holders. A live service would not rely on unapproved automated collection. Development would proceed only under a written arrangement covering:

  • the authorised data source and permitted purposes;
  • attribution, linking and presentation; and
  • appropriate privacy, correction and removal processes.

Easy Live Auction’s listing would remain the authoritative record. Members would always be directed there to verify descriptions, condition, estimates, fees and terms before acting.

Section 7

Founder and development approach

AuctionSavvy was conceived and built by Ken Gibbins, drawing on many years in the antique furniture trade and recent first-hand experience of buying through auctions. The morning briefing, urgency groupings, local emphasis and attention to collection practicality reflect real sourcing decisions rather than assumptions.

Ken developed the system using AI-assisted tools, demonstrating the working discovery journey from personalised match to the official Easy Live Auction listing. For production, appropriate engineering, data, security and user-experience expertise would be brought in, in consultation with Easy Live Auction, once the intended integration and scale were understood.

AuctionSavvy would remain founder-led, with Ken responsible for product direction and the buyer-side proposition. The commercial model — referral-based, membership-based or a combination — would be agreed once scope, investment and responsibilities were defined. One possible membership structure is sketched in Appendix A.

Section 8

A staged collaboration

A controlled pilot is proposed as the natural first stage of a longer collaboration — testing the proposition responsibly and improving it through evidence, without assuming success or failure in advance:

  1. Walkthrough and alignment — review the demonstration and discuss its fit with Easy Live Auction’s priorities.
  2. Data and technical discussion — establish what authorised access is feasible and what production would involve.
  3. Collaboration principles — agree catalogue control, branding, referrals, privacy and auctioneer relationships.
  4. Controlled pilot — work with a limited member group using jointly defined measures and scheduled review points.
  5. Progressive release — expand categories, membership and promotion as evidence and operational readiness support it.

Both parties retain the freedom to adjust or stop at any stage, while recognising that useful personalisation and buyer habits develop over more than a single short test. No public launch, commercial operation or broader data collection is proposed until the necessary permissions and arrangements are in place.

Appendix A

One possible membership model

Included for discussion only — not a commitment on pricing or final structure. A simple three-tier outline is proposed as a starting point; exact features, access, attribution and the commercial relationship with Easy Live Auction would all be agreed before development.

Level Proposed role Principal value
Free UK auction-house map, upcoming sale dates and a limited rotating Daily Showcase Demonstrates the discovery journey without personal searches or a complete personalised briefing
Collector Personalised morning briefing, Intelligent Featured Search, chosen interests and local houses, simple controls and auction reminders Saves serious private buyers routine research time and directs them to relevant official listings
Trade Collector features plus multiple wanted-item or client-sourcing briefs, AI-assisted matching, morning reports and a future AI Journey & Collection Planner Supports focused sourcing, customer requests and efficient auction and collection planning while returning buyers to Easy Live Auction

Possible Easy Live Auction customer benefit. Subject to agreement, AuctionSavvy could offer eligible existing Easy Live Auction customers a preferred introductory rate or trial period. This could support early adoption while giving Easy Live Auction an additional customer benefit to promote. Eligibility, duration, funding and commercial terms would be agreed together, ideally using a referral link or promotional code without sharing customer data.

Appendix B

Possible future directions

Later possibilities, not existing capabilities, partnerships or commitments.

Intelligent Featured Search

With authorised data, a broad interest could be expanded into transparent related concepts and used for meaning-based lot selection beyond exact keyword matches. Members could approve, remove or clarify the suggested interpretation rather than relying on an opaque AI decision.

Intelligent Refined Search and alerts

Specific natural-language requirements could be compared with incoming lots, including dimensions and close alternatives, with match explanations, human review and optional alerts.

AI Journey & Collection Planner

With authorised catalogue data and user-controlled memory, a conversational assistant could combine saved interests, active requirements and current commitments with travel plans, auction dates and collection windows. It could ask clarifying questions, identify worthwhile diversions or compatible collections, and revise the plan as circumstances or new opportunities change. Members would control and be able to delete anything the assistant remembers.

Delivery information

Auction-house options shown first, with possible introductions to specialist providers. Using a member’s stored postcode, an estimated delivery price range could appear automatically against each lot, helping buyers judge total cost before bidding; formal quotations would remain the provider’s responsibility.

Chrome companion development

Building on the working private companion, further carefully chosen browser-side features could be explored with Easy Live Auction’s agreement. These could make catalogue browsing clearer and help buyers continue their journey through Easy Live Auction.

Seller-to-auction-house introductions

A guided route helping prospective sellers identify relevant houses and continue to an official valuation or consignment inquiry.

Overseas buyers and UK buying tours

Curated discovery and trip-planning for overseas trade buyers ahead of sourcing trips, with realistic expectations on eligibility, payment, shipping and export.

Referral and relevance insight

Subject to privacy and commercial agreement, aggregate reporting on which discovery routes produce genuine engagement on Easy Live Auction.
